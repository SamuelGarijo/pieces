# Step 6a — the reference sweep (2026-09-06)

Method: `content-creation-engine/plugins/content-creation-engine/pipeline.md` §6a. This runs
**before** the drawing brief (`05-brief.md`). The script (`03-script.md`) names a rhetorical
figure per frame; a figure is a rule of meaning, not a picture. This file turns each figure
into a *visual problem* and says where to go looking for solutions — inside mymind first,
then by name outside it.

**The mymind queries live in one place: [§ Queries](#the-mymind-queries-fire-these) at the end
of this file.** The `mymind:` line inside each frame below names *objects* (a shelf, a form) —
those are the engine's first guesses at a form, and guessing the object is exactly what the
sweep is supposed to avoid. Search the **device**, not the object: not "shelves" but "two
things compared side by side, one full and one empty". The object is what the sweep decides.

**What to bring back:** 3–5 references per frame, saved to mymind with a note saying *what
it solves* (not what it is). One line is enough: "Becher — same frame repeated, difference
is the only content."

**Two rules that also govern the sweep:**

- **No photographs of victims**, and none collected as reference either (draft §8: doing so
  commits what the piece denounces). This is not a limitation to work around — it is the
  piece's formal problem, and §8 below names the artist who solved it.
- **No BBC faces, no editors, no author.** The institution appears through objects and
  sentences. Reference material of people-at-work is off-brief.

**Tag what you save** with the figure name from `visual-rhetoric.md` (`figure:antitesis`,
`figure:elipsis-iconica`, `figure:metonimia-propietario`…). That is the pre-sweep the
pipeline needs: the handbook's vocabulary becomes the archive's tagging vocabulary, and the
next piece starts with an archive that already answers "give me a juxtaposition".

## 0 — The plastic norm (sweep this first, once, for the whole piece)

Not a frame: the look that makes ten frames read as one piece. Script: newsprint grisaille,
`composicion-isotopica-regular`, **one accent colour reserved for the tag and nothing else**.

**The visual problem:** a grey, printed, documentary surface that still looks handmade — so
the piece reads as an essay, not as an infographic.

**mymind:** `newsprint texture` · `risograph one colour` · `grisalla` · `halftone grey`
· `two colour print` · `editorial grid magazine` · `papel prensa` · `duotone poster`

**Outside:** risograph editorial work (Colour Code, Hato Press catalogues) for how one
accent colour behaves on grey · Otl Aicher and Karl Gerstner for the regular grid ·
*Le Monde diplomatique*'s cover tradition (one idea, one image, no photograph of the event)
· Emil Ruder / Wolfgang Weingart for the typographic pair.

**Decide from the sweep:** which single accent colour, and whether the grey is a paper
texture or a flat fill. Both decisions propagate to all ten frames.

## 1 — Threshold. The law, as two shelves.

**Figures:** `antitesis`, `hiperbole-silencio`. **Symbol:** `hole` (planted).
**Visual problem:** two containers of the same kind, one full and one nearly empty, where
the *sameness of the frame* is what makes the difference legible — without a chart.

**mymind:** `estantería archivo` · `archive boxes` · `shelves library` · `grid missing
element` · `typology grid` · `inventory rows` · `repetition with a gap` · `casilleros`

**Outside:**
- **Bernd & Hilla Becher**, typologies (water towers, gas tanks) — the canonical answer to
  "identical frame, difference is the only content". The grid *is* the argument.
- **Christian Boltanski**, *Les Archives* / shelved tin boxes — archives as accumulation and
  as absence, without a single face.
- **Aby Warburg**, Mnemosyne panels — objects arranged so that adjacency argues.
- **Herbert Bayer**, *World Geo-Graphic Atlas* — quantity as arranged units.

**Watch for:** the gap must read as *removed*, not as *not yet filled*.

---

## 2 — The hook. Donbás: eight years, and nobody knows.

**Figures:** `elipsis-iconica`, `elipsis-fondo`. **Symbol:** `hole`.
**Visual problem:** the same shelf, now empty, floating — no wall, no floor. A void that
reads as *unrecorded*, not as *clean*. Composition runs right-to-left (it is a flashback).

**mymind:** `objeto flotando fondo blanco` · `isolated object no background` · `white void` ·
`empty room photograph` · `negative space poster` · `hueco` · `absence`

**Outside:**
- **Rachel Whiteread**, casts of interiors — the void made solid; the strongest available
  model for "the missing thing is the object".
- **Doris Salcedo**, *Shibboleth* / *Untitled* furniture — absence as a structural fault.
- **Noma Bar** — figure/ground where the empty shape is the subject (register is more
  commercial; useful for the *mechanic*, not the tone).
- **Gordon Matta-Clark**, building cuts — removal as an act.

**Watch for:** floating without ground must feel like *loss of context*, not like a product
shot. Shadow is the whole difference; collect examples of both.

## 3 — Pair 1. The same verb, and the other verb.

**Figures:** `antitesis`, `elipsis-iconica` (the agent). **Visual problem:** two headlines set
identically, one of which has a **hole where the subject should be** — and the reader fills
it. This is the frame that makes the piece's mechanism visible as form.

**mymind:** `erasure poetry` · `blackout poem` · `redacted document` · `texto tachado` ·
`censored text` · `headline typography poster` · `typographic pair` · `fill in the blank`

**Outside:**
- **Jenny Holzer**, the *Redaction Paintings* — declassified documents enlarged, the blanks
  intact. Closest existing work to this exact frame: a blacked-out official sentence, made
  monumental.
- **Tom Phillips**, *A Humument*, and **Jen Bervin**, *Nets* — erasure as a way of making a
  new sentence out of an old one.
- **Barbara Kruger** — headline typography that accuses. Use for the *voice*, not the palette;
  her red is the opposite of our one-reserved-colour rule.
- **Marcel Broodthaers**, *Un coup de dés* (the blacked-out Mallarmé) — the poem as bars.

**Watch for:** the blank must sit exactly where a subject sits in a normal sentence, and the
two headlines must be typographically indistinguishable — otherwise the antithesis leaks.

---

## 4 — Pair 2. The tag.

**Figures:** `metonimia-propietario`, `antitesis`. **Symbol:** `tag` (established here — the
piece's only colour appears for the first time).
**Visual problem:** a small label that changes what the thing under it means; and its twin,
bare. The label must look *administrative*, not editorial.

**mymind:** `sticker label object` · `etiqueta adhesiva` · `price tag` · `museum wall label` ·
`warning label packaging` · `rubber stamp overprint` · `archive stamp` · `certification seal`

**Outside:**
- **Hans Haacke**, *Shapolsky et al.* and the institutional-label works — the label as the
  content; the bureaucratic register done as art.
- **Cigarette-packet warning design** (the plain-packaging research, any jurisdiction) — an
  entire design discipline built on "a label that reframes the object it sits on".
- **Lawrence Weiner / Joseph Kosuth** — language placed on things as the work itself.
- **Library and customs stamps**, archival accession marks — the visual grammar of "someone
  processed this".

**Watch for:** the tag must be reusable at three sizes (frame 4 full, frames 5–8 corner-small,
frame 10 faint-or-absent). Collect references for the *small* state too; that is where it
usually fails.

---

## 5 — Pair 3. The guidance and the pages.

**Figures:** `antitesis`, `antifrasis`. **Visual problem:** a rule and its violation in the
same shot — one page that states the policy, one stack whose *height* is the count of times
it was ignored. Quantity as physical mass, not as a bar.

**mymind:** `stack of papers` · `pila de papeles` · `desk documents overhead` · `style guide
spread` · `manual page` · `printed repetition` · `ream of paper` · `accumulation of sheets`

**Outside:**
- **Hanne Darboven** — walls of written sheets; accumulation as duration made visible.
- **On Kawara** / **Roman Opałka** — counting as a body of work; the moral weight of a number
  you can see the size of.
- **Forensic Architecture** — document + annotation + measurement as a public argument. The
  closest working model for this piece as a whole; study how they annotate without shouting.
- **Taryn Simon**, *An American Index of the Hidden and Unfamiliar* — the object and its long
  caption as a single unit. Directly relevant to how caption and image share the load here.

**Watch for:** the stack must be countable-looking (edges legible), or the height reads as
generic "paperwork".

---

## 6 — Inset. The count.

**Figures:** `antitesis` (two bars), `hiperbole-escala`. **Visual problem:** a small, sharp
chart inset into a large soft field, so the eye stops. And: a chart that looks *drawn*, in a
hand-made essay, without becoming decorative.

**mymind:** `hand drawn chart` · `minimal bar chart poster` · `data visualization editorial` ·
`inset panel` · `small detail large surface` · `gráfico dibujado a mano`

**Outside:**
- **W. E. B. Du Bois**, the 1900 Paris data portraits — hand-drawn charts about racial
  injustice. The exact register this piece needs: moral argument, made by hand, in colour
  discipline. If one reference decides frames 6 and 7, it is this one.
- **Otto Neurath / Gerd Arntz**, Isotype — quantity as repeated units rather than length.
- **Giorgia Lupi** (*Dear Data*) and **Federica Fragapane** — contemporary handmade data.
- **Edward Tufte**, small multiples — for the inset's proportions, not its style.

**Watch for:** the Gaza bar carries the tag's colour. Check that against the accent colour
chosen in §0 — it must survive at bar width.

---

## 7 — Braid-return. The rupture.

**Figures:** `antitesis`, `autoironia`. **Visual problem:** the *same* image as frame 6 with
two more bars added — an image that corrects itself in front of the reader. The addition must
look like an annotation, not like a redesign.

**mymind:** `annotated chart` · `handwritten correction` · `marginalia red pen` ·
`proofreading marks` · `errata` · `before after same image` · `nota al margen`

**Outside:**
- **Proofreader's and copy-editing marks** — a whole notation system for "this is wrong,
  here is the fix", already legible to any reader.
- **W. E. B. Du Bois** again (same plate family as frame 6 — the braid depends on the two
  frames being visibly the same object).
- **Hans-Peter Feldmann** and **Sophie Calle**'s annotated photographs — the author writing on
  their own image.
- Scientific-figure convention: the **inset panel that rescales the same data**. Look at how
  journals mark "same data, different normalisation".

**Watch for:** frame 7 must be recognisably frame 6 at thumbnail size. If the two read as
different images, the surprise becomes a new topic instead of a correction.

---

## 8 — Beat. The form. *(the frame that decides the piece's ethics)*

**Figures:** `metonimia-soporte`, `metonimia-productor`. **Visual problem:** the mechanism as
an object — eight fanned sheets, a signature line, a stamp, a lanyard, seen from above, no
hands. The most textured frame; the pause before the landing. And, underneath: **how to
represent a war without showing a victim.**

**mymind:** `documents flat lay` · `official form stamp` · `formulario oficial` · `carbon
copy` · `typewritten form` · `press pass lanyard` · `bureaucratic paperwork` · `evidence
photography`

**Outside:**
- **Alfredo Jaar**, *Real Pictures* (1995) — photographs of Rwanda sealed inside black boxes,
  with the description printed on the lid; and *The Sound of Silence*. This is the definitive
  solved case of the piece's own constraint: the image withheld, and the withholding made
  into the form. **Look at this before drawing anything.**
- **Mike Mandel & Larry Sultan**, *Evidence* (1977) — found institutional photographs read as
  a sequence; the flat, procedural look this frame wants.
- **Taryn Simon**, *Contraband* — objects on a neutral ground, catalogued.
- **Simon Menner**, *Top Secret* (Stasi archive images) — bureaucracy photographing itself.

**Watch for:** texture is the point here (`textura-materica-impasta`). Collect paper surfaces,
not just layouts.

---

## 9 — Link. The rivals.

**Figure:** `acumulacion-iconica`. **Visual problem:** three objections, given as three small
neutral objects in a row — visibly *lesser* than the kernels, but not dismissive. The design
has to look fair.

**mymind:** `index cards row` · `fichas` · `three panels` · `footnote card` · `small
multiples` · `annotation cards` · `museum label triptych`

**Outside:**
- **Aby Warburg** panels again (adjacency as reasoning, at small scale).
- **Fact-check card formats** (any serious outlet's "what we can and can't say" box) — the
  register of an honest caveat.
- Museum **wall-label triptychs** — three short texts that carry equal weight.

**Watch for:** if these read as a footer, the fairness beat disappears. They are small but
not decorative.

---

## 10 — Terminal. The sentence.

**Figures:** `elipsis-iconica`, `repeticion-variacion`. **Symbols:** `hole` breaks, `tag`
breaks. **Visual problem:** frame 1's two shelves return as two lines of type; the second
line has the blank. The composition must *rhyme* with frame 1 at a glance, and end open.

**mymind:** `typographic poster two lines` · `fill in the blank` · `cloze` · `underscore
blank line` · `formulario en blanco` · `one line poster`

**Outside:**
- **Jenny Holzer**, *Truisms* / LED works — one line, public, unattributed, high stakes.
- **Barbara Kruger** — for the address to *you*, which is what the hook does.
- **Ferdinand Kriwet**, text works — typography as a public sign.
- The **blank line on a printed form** — rhymes with frame 8's paperwork, closing the loop
  between the mechanism and the reader's own reading.

**Decided (2026-09-06):** the blank stays **empty** — the tag does not fill it. The hole ends
unlabelled, and it becomes the reader's. Bring references anyway for the alternative; if the
sweep produces something that changes the mind, it changes before the brief, not after.

## Prompt 1 — `antitesis` (§3.19) · frames 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7

Fire at Claude with the mymind account. Tagging conventions and the reasoning behind the
shape live in `content-creation-engine/references/mymind-prompt-pack.md` — the prompt itself
stays short.

> **Search my mymind for `antitesis`** (Retórica de la pintura §3.19).
>
> I want images that work as a visual antithesis: two things of the same kind, built the
> same way, side by side, where the only difference is what they mean — and here,
> specifically, how much they hold. One full, one nearly empty. Scales with unequal loads;
> a series or grid with one bare cell; the same page or label twice with unequal content;
> before/after where the second state is a subtraction; two words or colours set as
> opposites in one composition.
>
> Not identical or mirrored pairs (that is `repeticion`). Not single dramatic images. No
> faces, no photographs of victims.
>
> Tag keepers `figure:antitesis` + `piece:asimetria-de-encuadre`, with one line each on what
> it *solves*, not what it shows. Anything else you pass, tag by its figure
> (`figure:repeticion`, `figure:elipsis`, `figure:metonimia`). Then tell me which object
> repeated across the hits.

**Frame 1 candidate (Samuel, 2026-09-06):** Isotype, *University Students and Population
aged 20-23* (Isotype Institute, 1932 data) — the same frame repeated, quantity as units, one
side almost empty. Antithesis and `hiperbole-escala` in one image. Saved as the working
reference for frame 1; it also serves frame 6.

## Prompt 2 — `elipsis` (§3.5) · frames 2, 3, 10

> **Search my mymind for `elipsis`** (Retórica de la pintura §3.5).
>
> I want images where something has been removed and its absence is the subject: a sentence
> with a word cut out; a document with a blank or a redaction; the outline, shadow or imprint
> of an object no longer there; a series with one slot empty; an object shown with no ground
> — no wall, no floor — floating out of context. The viewer should feel pushed to fill the
> gap.
>
> Not damage or decay; not minimalism for its own sake. No faces, no photographs of victims.
>
> Tag keepers `figure:elipsis` + `piece:asimetria-de-encuadre` (add `figure:elipsis-fondo`
> when it is the ground that is missing), one line each on what it solves. Tag what you pass
> by its figure. Tell me which object repeated.

## Prompt 3 — `metonimia` (§3.10) · frames 4, 8

> **Search my mymind for `metonimia`** (Retórica de la pintura §3.10).
>
> I want images where a part, a label or an object stands for a whole institution or a whole
> act: a sticker, stamp, tag or seal that changes what it sits on; an official form, a
> signature line, a lanyard, a rubber stamp — the paperwork instead of the people; anything
> that says "someone processed this". Bureaucracy shown by its things, with nobody in the
> picture.
>
> Not portraits, not people at desks. No faces.
>
> Tag keepers `figure:metonimia` + `piece:asimetria-de-encuadre` — `figure:metonimia-propietario`
> for the label kind, `figure:metonimia-soporte` for the document kind — one line each on
> what it solves. Tag what you pass by its figure. Tell me which object repeated.

## Prompt 4 — `hiperbole` (§3.18) · frames 1, 2, 6, 7

> **Search my mymind for `hiperbole`** — two kinds only: silence and scale.
>
> Silence: a frame that is almost empty on purpose — a blank field, a black square, one small
> thing in a lot of nothing — where the emptiness is an excess, not an unfinished design.
> Scale: a quantity made visible by size or by repeated units — rows of identical figures,
> tally marks, a small sharp chart inside a large soft field, hand-drawn bars where one is
> minute beside the other.
>
> Not caricature, not dramatic exaggeration of a person. No faces, no victims.
>
> Tag keepers `figure:hiperbole-silencio` or `figure:hiperbole-escala` + `piece:asimetria-de-encuadre`,
> one line each on what it solves. Tag what you pass by its figure. Tell me which object
> repeated.

## Prompt 5 — `ironia` (§3.16: antífrasis and autoironía) · frames 5, 7

> **Search my mymind for `ironia`** — two kinds: antiphrasis and self-irony.
>
> Antiphrasis: a rule and its breach in one shot — a sign beside what it forbids, a manual
> next to the thing done wrong, a promise and its evidence side by side, with no comment.
> Self-irony: an image that corrects itself — handwriting over a printed page, proofreader's
> marks, a note in the margin, a chart with a second panel that undoes the first, an author
> annotating their own work.
>
> Not sarcasm, not memes, not jokes at someone else's expense.
>
> Tag keepers `figure:antifrasis` or `figure:autoironia` + `piece:asimetria-de-encuadre`, one
> line each on what it solves. Tag what you pass by its figure. Tell me which object repeated.

## Prompt 6 — `acumulacion` (§3.2) · frames 5, 9

> **Search my mymind for `acumulacion`** (Retórica de la pintura §3.2).
>
> I want images where many things of one kind are gathered so that the quantity, not any
> single item, is the message: a stack of paper whose height is a count; a wall of sheets; a
> row of index cards; small panels in sequence carrying equal weight; catalogues of objects
> laid out flat.
>
> Not clutter — the pile has to read as a count. No faces.
>
> Tag keepers `figure:acumulacion` + `piece:asimetria-de-encuadre`, one line each on what it
> solves. Tag what you pass by its figure. Tell me which object repeated.

searched once.

**How to run it:** one query at a time; keep what answers the *device*, discard what merely
matches the words. Save each keeper with the figure tag (`figure:antitesis`,
`figure:elipsis-iconica`, `figure:metonimia-propietario`…) and a one-line note on what it
solves.

## Output of this step

A folder of saved references (in mymind, tagged by figure) plus, per frame, a one-line note
on what the chosen reference solves. Then `05-brief.md` gets written from the script **plus**
these — composition, gutters, transitions, texture, palette.
