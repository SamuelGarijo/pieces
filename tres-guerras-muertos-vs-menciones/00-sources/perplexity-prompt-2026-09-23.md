# Perplexity research prompt — three wars, deaths vs coverage (2026-09-23)

Paste as one message. Ask follow-ups per conflict if it compresses. The point is
**comparable numbers**: same window, same outlets, same unit — not three unrelated
totals.

---

I'm building a three-panel chart comparing three current wars — **Ukraine (since 24 Feb
2022), Gaza (since 7 Oct 2023) and Sudan (since 15 Apr 2023)** — on two axes: people
killed, and how much Western media covered them. I need numbers that can sit side by
side honestly. For each of the three conflicts, give me:

1. **Deaths.** The best *verified* count (OHCHR / OCHA / Gaza MoH / ACLED / UN) and the
   best *estimate* (e.g. LSHTM, Lancet, US envoy figures for Sudan), each with source,
   date of the figure, method, and whether it is civilians only or all deaths. Say
   plainly which numbers are confirmed and which are modelled.
2. **Coverage.** Any study that counted mentions, articles, TV segments or minutes for
   these conflicts in the *same* outlets over the *same* window — GDELT TV Explorer,
   Media Cloud, CfMM, The Nation's CNN+MSNBC count (Johnson & Ali, Oct 2024), Pew,
   Reuters Institute, CARE's "Breaking the Silence" least-covered-crises reports. If no
   study covers all three, give me the closest pairs and say what's missing. Prefer
   "first 100 days" and "first 12 months" windows.
3. **The ratio.** Where possible, mentions per 1,000 deaths, computed from the numbers
   above, with the arithmetic shown.
4. **Economic exposure of the EU/Europe** to each conflict — energy, wheat/food prices,
   trade, migration — with a source per claim. I want to test whether coverage tracks
   economic impact.
5. **The February 2022 quotes** about Ukrainian refugees being "relatively civilised,
   relatively European", "they look like us", "not a developing, third-world nation" —
   exact wording, outlet, journalist, date, and the AMEJA statement that answered them.
6. Any quantified work on Herman & Chomsky's "worthy and unworthy victims" applied to
   these three conflicts.

Rules: primary sources with URLs; label every number as verified / estimate /
secondary; flag contradictions between sources instead of averaging them; no
percentages without the raw counts behind them.
